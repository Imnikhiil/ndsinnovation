SEO Basics Every Business Owner Should Know

You don't need to be a tech expert to understand SEO. Here's a no-jargon guide to getting your business found on Google — written specifically for Indian entrepreneurs and small business owners.

What Is SEO, Really?

SEO stands for Search Engine Optimization. In simple terms, it's the practice of making your website show up when someone searches for something related to your business on Google. If you run a bakery in Dwarka and someone types "best bakery near Dwarka," SEO is what decides whether your website appears on page 1 or page 10.

Think of Google as a librarian. When someone asks a question, Google looks through billions of web pages and picks the ones it thinks are the most relevant and trustworthy. SEO is how you tell Google, "Hey, my page is exactly what this person is looking for."

There's no magic trick involved. SEO is a combination of writing useful content, structuring your website properly, and building trust over time. Let's break it down step by step.

Keywords: The Foundation of SEO

Keywords are the words and phrases people type into Google. If you sell handmade candles, your keywords might be "handmade candles online India," "scented candles Delhi," or "buy soy candles."

How to Find the Right Keywords

  • Google Autocomplete: Start typing your product or service into Google and see what it suggests. Those suggestions are real searches people make every day.
  • Google's "People Also Ask" box: Scroll down on any search results page to find related questions. These are goldmines for blog content.
  • Free tools: Use Ubersuggest or Google Keyword Planner (free with a Google Ads account) to see search volume and competition for any keyword.
  • Think like your customer: Don't use industry jargon. A customer won't search "bespoke confectionery" — they'll search "custom birthday cake near me."

Once you have a list of 10–20 keywords, use them naturally in your page titles, headings, and body text. Don't stuff them in — Google is smart enough to penalize that.

Meta Titles and Descriptions

Every page on your website has a meta title and meta description. These are the blue link and the small text you see on Google's search results page. Most business owners ignore these completely, which is a huge missed opportunity.

  • Meta title: Keep it under 60 characters. Include your main keyword and your brand name. Example: "Handmade Soy Candles in Delhi | Aromalight"
  • Meta description: Keep it under 155 characters. Write a compelling one-liner that makes people want to click. Example: "Shop premium handmade soy candles with free delivery across Delhi NCR. 100% natural, eco-friendly."

Think of the meta title as your shop's signboard and the meta description as the person standing outside inviting customers in. They don't directly boost your ranking, but a good description dramatically increases clicks — and more clicks signal to Google that your result is relevant.

Page Speed Matters More Than You Think

If your website takes more than 3 seconds to load, over half your visitors will leave before they even see your content. Google knows this, and it uses page speed as a ranking factor.

Common reasons for slow websites in India:

  • Uncompressed images (uploading 5MB photos directly from your phone)
  • Using heavy WordPress themes with 20 plugins
  • Cheap hosting with servers located outside India
  • No caching enabled

Quick fixes: compress images using TinyPNG before uploading, choose a lightweight theme, enable browser caching, and consider hosting on platforms that have Indian or Asian servers. Test your speed for free at PageSpeed Insights by Google — aim for a score above 80 on mobile.

Local SEO: Your Secret Weapon

If you run a local business — a restaurant, salon, clinic, tuition centre, or shop — local SEO is the single most important thing you can do online. And it starts with Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business).

Setting Up Google Business Profile

  1. Go to business.google.com and claim your business.
  2. Fill out every single field — address, phone number, website, hours, category, services.
  3. Upload at least 10 high-quality photos of your business, products, and team.
  4. Ask happy customers to leave Google reviews. Reviews are the number one ranking factor for local search.
  5. Post updates weekly — share offers, new products, or behind-the-scenes content.

When someone searches "web developer near me" or "best salon in Rohini," Google pulls results from Business Profiles first. If yours is incomplete, you're invisible.

Mobile Optimization Is Non-Negotiable

Over 75% of internet users in India browse on their phones. Google uses mobile-first indexing, which means it looks at the mobile version of your website first when deciding rankings.

What mobile optimization looks like:

  • Text is readable without zooming
  • Buttons are large enough to tap easily
  • No horizontal scrolling
  • Images resize properly on smaller screens
  • Navigation menu works on mobile (hamburger menu)

If your website looks broken on a phone, fix it before doing anything else SEO-related. Nothing else matters if 3 out of 4 visitors can't even use your site.

Internal Linking: Connect Your Pages

Internal links are links from one page of your website to another page on the same website. For example, if you write a blog about "best candle scents for bedrooms," you should link to your product page for bedroom candles within the blog.

Why does this matter?

  • It helps Google discover and understand all the pages on your site.
  • It keeps visitors on your site longer, reducing bounce rate.
  • It passes "link equity" from popular pages to newer pages, boosting their rankings.

A good rule of thumb: every page should have at least 2–3 internal links pointing to it. Your blog is the perfect place to build these links naturally.

Common SEO Myths — Debunked

"SEO is a one-time thing."

False. SEO is an ongoing process. Google updates its algorithm hundreds of times a year. Your competitors are also optimizing. You need to consistently publish content, update old pages, and monitor your rankings.

"More keywords = higher ranking."

False. Keyword stuffing was a tactic from 2010. Today, Google's AI can understand context and synonyms. Write for humans, not robots. If your content reads awkwardly because you forced a keyword in 15 times, you're hurting yourself.

"Paid ads improve your SEO."

False. Google Ads and organic search are completely separate. Running ads won't boost your organic rankings. However, ads can give you visibility while you build your organic presence — they work together, not as replacements.

"Small businesses can't compete with big brands in SEO."

False. This is the best part. With local SEO, niche keywords, and consistent content, a small business in Laxmi Nagar can outrank a national chain for local searches. Google actually favors relevance and proximity for local queries.

Where to Start Today

You don't need to do everything at once. Here's a simple action plan:

  1. Week 1: Claim and fully complete your Google Business Profile.
  2. Week 2: Check your website's mobile-friendliness and page speed. Fix any issues.
  3. Week 3: Write meta titles and descriptions for your top 5 pages.
  4. Week 4: Publish your first blog post targeting a keyword your customers actually search for.

SEO is a marathon, not a sprint. But the businesses that start today will be miles ahead of those that keep putting it off. Your website is your most powerful salesperson — make sure people can actually find it.
