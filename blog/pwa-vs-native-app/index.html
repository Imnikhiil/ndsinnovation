PWA vs Native App — Which One Should You Build?

You want an app for your business. But should you spend ₹5 lakhs on a native app, or get a PWA for a fraction of the cost? Let's break down the real differences.

Every week, I get at least one inquiry that starts with: "I want to build an app." And almost every time, my first question is: "Do you actually need a native app, or would a PWA work better?"

Most people don't know the difference. And honestly, a lot of agencies exploit that confusion to sell you something expensive when you don't need it. So let me give you the honest, no-BS breakdown.

What Is a Native App?

A native app is the kind of app you download from the Google Play Store or Apple App Store. It's built using platform-specific languages — Kotlin or Java for Android, Swift for iOS. It lives on your phone's home screen and has deep access to your device's hardware.

Examples you use daily: WhatsApp, Zomato, Paytm, Instagram. These are all native apps (or close to it — some use hybrid frameworks like React Native or Flutter, but the end result is similar).

Native apps can access:

  • Camera and microphone
  • GPS and location services
  • Contacts and phone dialer
  • Bluetooth and NFC
  • Push notifications (with full control)
  • Offline storage and background processes

What Is a PWA?

A Progressive Web App (PWA) is essentially a website that behaves like an app. It runs in your browser but can be "installed" on your home screen. It looks and feels like a native app — full screen, splash screen, smooth animations — but it's built with standard web technologies: HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

Some well-known PWAs: Twitter Lite, Pinterest, Starbucks, MakeMyTrip, Flipkart Lite. Yes, Flipkart — one of India's biggest e-commerce companies — uses a PWA alongside their native app.

What a PWA can do:

  • Work offline (caching key pages and data)
  • Send push notifications (on Android and desktop; limited on iOS)
  • Be installed on the home screen without going to an app store
  • Load incredibly fast, even on slow 3G connections
  • Auto-update without user action

Cost Comparison: Where the Real Difference Lies

Let's talk money — because for most businesses, especially startups and small businesses in India, this is the deciding factor.

Native App

  • Development cost: ₹2,00,000 – ₹15,00,000+ (depending on complexity)
  • Timeline: 3-6 months minimum
  • Maintenance: ₹20,000 – ₹50,000/month for updates, bug fixes, server costs
  • App store fees: ₹2,100/year (Google Play) + ₹8,500/year (Apple Developer)
  • Need separate builds: One for Android, one for iOS (unless using cross-platform)

PWA

  • Development cost: ₹15,000 – ₹1,50,000
  • Timeline: 2-6 weeks
  • Maintenance: ₹2,000 – ₹10,000/month (hosting + minor updates)
  • App store fees: None (it's a website)
  • One build works everywhere: Android, iOS, desktop — all from a single codebase

For most small-to-medium businesses, a PWA delivers 80% of the native app experience at 20% of the cost.

Performance: How Do They Compare?

Native apps are undeniably faster for heavy, complex tasks. If you're building a gaming app, a video editing tool, or something that relies heavily on device hardware (like a fitness tracker using Bluetooth sensors), native is the way to go.

But for content-driven apps, e-commerce stores, service booking platforms, restaurant menus, and business directories? A PWA performs just as well. In many cases, it performs better because:

  • PWAs are lighter — typically under 1 MB, compared to native apps that can be 50-200 MB
  • Instant loading with service workers caching assets
  • No app store friction — users don't need to download anything; they just visit a URL
  • Lower data consumption — critical for Indian users on limited data plans
When Flipkart launched Flipkart Lite (their PWA), they saw a 70% increase in conversions. Pinterest's PWA increased engagement by 60%. Twitter Lite reduced data usage by 70%.

When Should You Choose a PWA?

A PWA is the right choice when:

  • You're a small business, freelancer, or early-stage startup with a limited budget
  • Your app is primarily content or service-based (menus, portfolios, booking systems, catalogs)
  • You want to reach users on all platforms without building separate apps
  • Speed and SEO matter — PWAs are indexable by Google, native apps are not
  • Your target users are in India, where data costs and storage space on phones are real concerns
  • You need something live in weeks, not months

When Should You Choose a Native App?

Go native when:

  • Your app requires deep hardware access (Bluetooth, NFC, advanced camera features, AR/VR)
  • You're building a complex product like a ride-sharing app, social network, or fintech platform
  • You need advanced push notification controls on iOS
  • You have the budget for ongoing maintenance, updates, and app store management
  • Your business model depends on the app being in the Play Store/App Store for discoverability
  • You need background processing (like a music player or fitness tracker)

The Installation Process: PWA Wins on Simplicity

Here's something most people don't realize: the average user doesn't want to download another app. Phone storage is precious, especially on budget smartphones (which dominate the Indian market).

With a native app, the journey looks like this: See an ad → Open Play Store → Search for the app → Wait for download (50-100 MB) → Open the app → Create an account. That's at least 5 steps and 2-3 minutes. Most people drop off midway.

With a PWA, it's: Tap a link → The app opens instantly → Optional: tap "Add to Home Screen." That's it. Under 5 seconds. No download, no storage concerns, no friction.

For businesses that rely on quick conversions — restaurants, service providers, event organizers — this difference is massive.

Real-World Examples in India

Flipkart Lite (PWA)

MakeMyTrip (PWA)

OLX (PWA)

These are not small companies experimenting with PWAs for fun. These are billion-dollar businesses that chose PWAs because the results speak for themselves.

The Verdict: Start With a PWA

If you're unsure, start with a PWA. Here's why:

  1. Lower risk — Less money invested upfront
  2. Faster to market — Launch in weeks, not months
  3. Validate your idea — See if users actually want your product before investing in native
  4. Easy to upgrade — If your PWA takes off, you can always build a native app later with real user data guiding your decisions

Most businesses that come to me asking for a native app actually need a PWA. And the ones who listen save lakhs of rupees while getting to market months faster.
